# How to make a natural-sounding hackathon video on your Windows laptop

You should not submit an unintelligible synthetic voice. A calm recording in **your own voice** is clearer, more credible and costs nothing.

1. Open `assets/kinship-demo.mp4` (the version with captions and no audio).
2. Open Microsoft Clipchamp (if installed on your laptop) or any video editor that supports voiceover.
3. Add the video to a new 16:9 timeline.
4. Record the narration from `docs/NARRATION_SCRIPT.md` in eight separate clips so you can position them at the timestamps shown. Use a headset or your iPhone voice recorder in a quiet room. Keep your mouth approximately 15–20 cm away from the mic.
5. Place each clip on the audio track at the stated time. Lower any background music to silence; clarity is most important.
6. Export as 720p or 1080p MP4 (H.264 + AAC). Watch the entire result with headphones. Upload to YouTube as Public or Unlisted only if event rules accept it (current rules specify *public* visibility, so use Public).

**Optional automated version:** `assets/kinship-demo-narrated.mp4` has slower, filtered speech. It's still an offline synthetic voice; it cannot sound like a professional human narrator in the current offline environment.

**Fastest workflow:** Use the included 68-second silent video, and make a single natural voice recording from the eight-sentence script above. This is more likely to sound professional than running another cheap robotic TTS engine.
